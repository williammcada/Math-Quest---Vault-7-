## Story

Merrow is a fictional occupied island in a WWII-era fictional conflict. The occupation's aircraft designer has left a prototype called Kestrel and warning of a planned attack on civilian evacuation boats. Adult crew characters: pilot Ada Vale, mechanic Oren, radio operator Mara, absent designer Dr. Ellis. No historical faction claims, national insignia or graphic violence.

Briefing → Silent Frequency → crew vote: retrieve Mara from the lighthouse OR recover Ellis's records → Bearings in the Dark → optional teacher-selected inserted story gates (The Harbor Ledger; A Signal Beyond the Reef) → Before the Propellers Turn → equipment preparation → five-minute individual escort flight → The Aircraft We Leave Behind → epilogue.

Early choice has explicit narrative consequences: Mara can verify safe passage and maintain the coastal warning network; recovered records preserve discoveries and evidence but Mara evacuates independently and cannot be confirmed aboard. No hidden arcade advantage for a moral choice. Both paths retain the essential convoy warning and access to all final choices.

Final options: deliver the prototype to allied military command (future protection, military control); trade it to the occupying command for witnessed civilian safe passage (immediate rescue, adversary gains technology); broadcast its discoveries and destroy it after landing (distributed knowledge, aircraft lost, future use beyond the crew's control). Six route × ending combinations have coherent additional paragraphs. All students vote, regardless of action outcome.

The students' independent arcade flights portray escort sorties clearing a corridor for Ada's separate prototype ferry. This avoids a destroyed player plane contradicting the final decision about a surviving prototype. Success, lesser escape, defeat, guided completion and technical closure remain personal engagement evidence; none changes math mastery or removes voting rights. A neutral narrative bridge handles no successful escort without inventing an arcade victory.

## Mathematics, equipment and lifecycle

Reuse MathQuest's 3/4/5 story gates, teacher topic selection, individual evidence, first-attempt scoring, participation rules, rotating Event Lead, voting/tie rules, extensions and reports. Required preparation earns one shared aircraft upgrade; up to two optional extra math blocks earn additional slots. Reuse agility, reinforced hull and damage upgrades and their exact flight effects. Each member must complete the prescribed block; the teacher can set its question count. Equal gate loads can supply the initial count; unequal gate loads require explicit teacher configuration before extra upgrades are offered. Preserve imported-bank reuse opt-in.

One issued flight per student, three internal lives and existing midpoint/boss checkpoints. Cumulative active max 300 seconds; checkpoint rewind never restores time. Existing integration contract has a separate 300-second authoritative crew window starting when equipment is committed; late starts, local pauses and disconnects do not extend it. Teacher whole-session pause freezes it. Notify players of this distinction. All records terminal or crew-window expiry opens final vote. Teacher can close flight neutrally. No early rescue minigame and no extra math between internal lives.

Authenticated run ownership, issued configuration, monotonic sequences/time/lives, terminal idempotency, reconnect, teacher pause/closure, expiry alarms and privacy-scoped local recovery are required. A disconnected client pauses after 30 seconds; a server-closed flight cannot be restarted or resurrected. Client-reported engagement is envelope-validated, not anti-cheat simulation or academic evidence.
