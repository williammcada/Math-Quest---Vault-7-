## 3. Proposed pacing contract: unlimited retries, one bounded window

**Recommended rule: unlimited retries within one server-controlled, five-minute team rescue window. Never five minutes per life.**

This is the proposed implementation of NF-EARLY-09 and NF-EARLY-10. The user supplied the time limit and lives requirement; shared stage-opening timing and the detailed lifecycle below are the recommended resolution.

### 3.1 Window start and authority

On the server's accepted resolution of the first team vote, lock the chosen route, open the early-rescue stage once, issue each rostered student a distinct personal rescue record, and record a shared deadline 300 seconds after stage opening. Prefetch the compact map/assets during the preceding gate/decision where practical. Do not delay the team's clock indefinitely waiting for every device to acknowledge readiness.

The timer includes briefing overlays, short dialogue, death/respawn animations, local pause, a backgrounded/locked device, time spent disconnected, and delayed Start. These are not opportunities to acquire extra classroom game time.

Preserve intentional teacher pause separately: only the teacher's authorized whole-session pause may freeze the window. Resume must account for that pause once, not add time per client or per retry. The teacher can explicitly close this early stage if needed. No student control extends the window.

Persist the deadline and phase identity on the authoritative service. Do not base permission to continue on a decrementing browser variable or on a student-supplied time. Refresh, Back/Forward, reconnect, repeated Start, repeated vote resolution, switching input mode, and retry must not create another window.

### 3.2 Personal success, retries, and team advancement

A player's rescue succeeds only when both the living player and the recruited survivor reach the terminal return zone. Reaching the bus without the survivor is not success. Defeating all zombies is never an objective prerequisite.

On success, close that player's rescue record and remove live replay/free-roam access to the chapter. Show a short safe completion panel with the next objective. Do not reward finishing early with an unlimited replay loop.

Preserve the shared team gate model: open Gate 2 early when all applicable rescue records are resolved, or unconditionally resolve unfinished records when the five-minute window closes. A successful student may wait briefly for the crew; that wait is bounded by the same deadline. Do not silently introduce individualized Gate 2 progress while other members remain in the prior stage. Such a change would need a separate academic-state design.

A student can intentionally consume the allocated five minutes. This policy does not claim to detect intent or eliminate that incentive; it prevents intentional deaths from extending the activity beyond the allocated window. No new punitive death limit or extra mathematics penalty is proposed.

At expiry, stop remaining gameplay and close unresolved rescue records even if players are dead, inside dialogue, still loading, disconnected, or never started. Move the team to the Gate 2 transition once. Do not wait for absent-device acknowledgements or an Event Lead click. Returning devices must reconcile to the closed stage instead of reopening the scene.

Ordinary Gate 2 participation requirements still apply. This action-stage deadline neither answers questions nor bypasses required mathematics.

### 3.3 Student-facing timer wording

Proposed start text:

> Reach the survivor and bring them back to the station. You can retry while the rescue timer is running. A retry does not restart the timer.

Always display **Rescue time left: M:SS**. A late starter sees the remaining shared time, not a fresh 5:00. At zero, no Retry, Continue dialogue, or Start control can reopen the chapter.

## 4. Compact mission and exact encounter inventory

### 4.1 Proposed map arrangement

Use one short, deliberately authored map topology, with two visually distinct rescue-building variants:

**Bus terminal → south road → alley → north road → selected rescue building → return by the same connected streets/alley → bus terminal.**

This arrangement is a proposal implementing the user's route description. Exact tile dimensions and travel distances require a playable layout check. Target a competent first attempt of roughly two to three minutes, with the remaining five-minute budget available for learning/retries; that duration target is not a measured result.

| Area | Fixed requested contents | Recommended placement considerations |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Bus terminal | Starting and return area | Clearly recognizable bus/terminal, visible return zone, short control prompt, safe spawn. |
| South road | Five zombies; one gas tank | Use the existing readable GAS/fuel hazard behavior. Provide space to activate it safely and a route around it. |
| Alley | Connecting passage | No additional authored zombies required. Recommended site for the one health pickup, without blocking movement. |
| North road | Ten zombies; one alarm car | The car's ready-state red blink is visible before activation. Put it where a diversion can help with the rescue entrance, not at an unreachable dead end. |
| Rescue entrance | Five additional zombies outside the building | These are additional to the ten north-road zombies. Leave meaningful routes between doorway, alarm, and windows. |
| Rescue interior | One selected survivor and short dialogue | Clinic or depot-garage identity; no battery pickup, shotgun, vest, or extra supply cache. |
| One reachable side recess | One ammo pickup | Recommended placement along the north-road approach, not behind an unavoidable fight. Exact placement is tuning. |

Twenty zombies is the authored initial population in each route variant/attempt. They may move between zones during play; the counts specify initial placement, not invisible leashes. Do not add the main city's ambient population, garage-battery horde, escape ambush, or respawn waves. If a complete attempt is restarted, reset the original twenty rather than accumulating more.

Use a chapter-specific population/configuration. Do not let the final game's global threat presets silently replace the requested 5/10/5 counts or introduce extra enemies. Enemy types and exact aggression were not specified; ordinary baseline zombies are the recommended starting proposal. Preserve the later game's separate threat settings.

### 4.2 Starting resources and pickups

Start with the ordinary pistol and **15 rounds**, with no Carbine, Ammo Pouch, Vest, shotgun, or inherited inventory advantage. The explicit health and ammunition pickups are the only supply exceptions.

Initial health, health-pickup amount, and ammo-pickup amount were not specified. Suggested first tuning: the current three-health player baseline, one health pickup restoring one point up to the normal cap, and one ammo pickup containing ten rounds. These three values are proposals, not user-approved constants.

No random drops, kill rewards, equipment slots, persistent credits, or imported final-run supplies. Rescue interaction itself must not inherit the sporting-goods-store healing reward. Only the specified health pickup and the defined retry policy restore health.

The gas tank is the one requested environmental hazard, not a collectable fuel objective. Use the established visible warning, fire/impact sounds, collision, and player-risk behavior without introducing a fuel-repair puzzle here.

The north-road alarm car uses the accepted touch/shot trigger, visible ready red blink, active flashing, full-duration sound, and enemy diversion. All living enemies in this small chapter respond. There is no garage-assault exception here, because this chapter has no scripted battery horde.

### 5.2 Pattern reused from the sporting goods store

Recommended interpretation of “modeled on the sporting goods store”:

- Guarded, locked front doorway, with the five requested entrance zombies.
- Two breakable side windows as usable entries/exits.
- Breaking a window attracts the local guards to that side; the car alarm provides a diversion and retains targeting priority during its active effect.
- The player enters, interacts with the survivor, and then escorts them out through a genuinely traversable route.

The user requested the precedent, but did not independently enumerate the new building's door/window count. Those details are explicitly the recommended reuse, not new purported quotations from the user.

Do not copy the store shotgun, its eight-shell reward, optional-survivor healing, late ambush reduction, or its narrative identity. The later sporting-goods-store rescue remains a different person and optional.

### 5.3 Escort behavior

The survivor follows behind the player after the short dialogue. The player still handles navigation, shooting, running and interaction; no second-character controls are required.

The requested immunity is to zombie damage. Recommended safeguard: also prevent friendly-fire and gas/fire damage to this noncombatant, so an environmental accident cannot destroy the narrative-required escort. This broader immunity is a proposal. The survivor must not shoot, kill, heal on demand, absorb bullets for the player, pin zombies, or become a solid shield.

Use valid follower navigation through broken windows, alley turns, door gaps and the return zone. Never force the player to restart because a follower is wedged on decorative collision. Any catch-up correction must use an already reached, traversable point near the player; it must not teleport the survivor from the rescue building to the terminal or satisfy the escort objective remotely.

Do not introduce extra return-wave zombies merely to create pressure. Use the surviving enemies and the consequences of the outward route/distraction.

## 6. Retry behavior — recommended checkpoint design

The unlimited-lives exception belongs **only to this early chapter**. It does not consume, grant or replenish attempts in the later full city mission.

Suggested two safe checkpoints:

1. Before recruitment: terminal/start checkpoint.
2. After recruitment/dialogue: safe rescue-interior checkpoint, with the survivor already following.

On player death, show a brief nonterminal “Try again — timer still running” state, then restore the relevant checkpoint while time remains. The dialogue should not replay in full on every return-leg retry.

Checkpoint recovery must restore a coherent world/resource snapshot, not selectively keep kills while refilling all ammunition and pickups. At the start checkpoint, reset the full attempt to its original population, objects, pickups and 15-round starting allowance. At the rescue checkpoint, restore the captured escort-phase world and ammunition/pickup state; full player health on retry is proposed. Do not accumulate pickups across retries. Zero remaining ammunition must not make the escort route physically impossible; running/diversion/avoidance remain valid tactics.

Respawn delay and brief safe-spawn protection are tuning proposals; both consume the same time window and cannot lengthen it. No automatic respawn after expiry or success.

A refresh is recovery of the existing attempt, not a fresh baseline loadout. Deliberate retry and network reconnect are distinct events. Authorize a retry only for a recorded/validated downed attempt; stale packets from the prior attempt must not mutate the current one.
