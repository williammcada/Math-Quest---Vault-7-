## 4. Car alarms: sustained sound and sustained diversion

### Required behavior

Alarm-equipped cars activate on either player contact with the car or a gunshot hitting it. Existing Search / Use activation may remain as an additional method. Mere proximity is not contact. Non-alarm cars remain non-alarm cars.

An active alarm produces a repeating car-alarm sound and synchronized visible flashing for the entire effect. Audio and AI read the same authoritative effect timer. Preserve the existing **12-second duration** unless a separate balance change is approved.

All living mobs disengage from the player, including mobs already chasing, winding up an attack, or lunging. Newly spawned mobs also obey an active alarm. A mob that sees the player during the effect must not immediately overwrite the alarm target with a chase target. Taking damage may animate a reaction, but must not cancel the diversion rule. No automatic player-directed contact/lunge damage should leak from an old attack after it is interrupted.

Mobs move toward reachable positions around the alarm car, not through walls or into its solid center. Mobs blocked by geometry continue pursuing the alarm target rather than reverting to the player during the effect. On expiry, normal perception resumes; the player is not located through walls by stale targeting information.

Mute affects playback only, never attraction. Pause freezes the remaining effect and its audio together. Resume restores the remaining effect, not a fresh full charge. End-of-run, restart and scene destruction stop the loop.

### Source-backed defects

`simulation.js` gives direct visual detection priority over sound investigation. Its shared noise record can also be replaced by shots/repairs, while ongoing distractions only refresh that record under limited conditions. The bullet loop explicitly activates barrels, not cars; alarms currently rely on the interaction action. `GameAudio.effect()` uses short atlas slices, not a persistent alarm loop.

### Proposed implementation choices

Use independent persistent alarm records and a higher-priority alarm-target state, rather than the single transient noise record. Reserve alarm playback independently of the short-effect voice cap so footsteps cannot silence it.

Keep the existing single-use-per-car behavior. Repeated touching or pellet hits must not restart the timer indefinitely. For overlapping alarms, use the most recently activated still-active alarm as the shared target, falling back to another live alarm when necessary. These concurrency details are proposals, not previously approved balance rules.

Detect bullet contact with the car before discarding the shot as blocked by the car's solid geometry. Use the actual car footprint and segment collision, not a small hidden center point.

## 6. Garage battery ambush

The trigger is successful completion of **collect the garage battery**. It is not collecting Dispatch keys, entering the garage, restoring power, or installing the battery at the bus.

### Immediate spawn and scripted ingress

Spawn the horde **very close to the garage doorway**, immediately outside/south of the entrance, at the moment the battery pickup completes. The group must become visible to the player almost immediately; do not place it far down the street or require normal wandering/investigation before it approaches.

The horde begins in a dedicated scripted **garage-assault / ingress state**. During this initial state:

- no line-of-sight check is required;
- the group does not need to “discover” the player;
- enemies immediately move **northward through the garage entrance** toward a fixed interior ingress point aligned with the battery/player area;
- once they cross into the garage interior, they continue directly toward the player rather than dropping into ordinary wandering/search behavior;
- the purpose is to create immediate escape pressure: the player should have to reach a window quickly unless they choose to fight.

The initial ingress must not be defeated by ordinary perception-state transitions. The horde must not spawn, hesitate, turn away, or wait to see the player before entering.

### Door / shutter breach

If the garage doorway/shutter is already open, the horde enters immediately.

If the doorway/shutter is closed for any reason when the horde arrives, the horde must **break it down immediately** and enter. Do not use the ordinary slow door-breach timer for this scripted event. The closed entrance should survive only long enough to communicate impact visually/audibly; it is not intended to buy the player meaningful extra time.

The breach must:

- visibly destroy/open the blocking door or shutter;
- remove collision immediately when the breach completes;
- avoid leaving an invisible wall;
- use a strong impact/breach animation where practical;
- play a **loud bang / crash audio cue** so the player instantly understands that something has hit the garage entrance.

If the entrance is already open, still provide a strong ambush cue at trigger time—such as the same loud bang/crash combined with zombie vocalization—so the event is unmistakable.

### Combat versus escape

The expected response is rapid escape through a garage window. However, the player is not forced to flee. A heavily equipped player with substantial ammunition and/or the sporting-goods-store shotgun may attempt to stand and fight.

Do not artificially make the horde invulnerable. The tension should come from proximity, numbers and immediate aggression.

### Alarm interaction

This scripted ingress is intended to guarantee the garage ambush. Therefore the **initial doorway rush has priority over ordinary perception and distraction rules until the group has breached/crossed into the garage**.

After the horde has entered the garage, normal global car-alarm diversion rules apply again: if an alarm is active, living mobs can be pulled away by it.

This is a deliberate exception to the otherwise global “active alarm overrides all mobs” rule so that the battery pickup always produces the intended immediate garage pressure.

### Persistence and duplication

Trigger once per run. Persist event completion and individual enemy states; restoring a snapshot must not duplicate or resurrect the group. Do not reinterpret an old Dispatch-horde flag as proof the new garage event has happened without an explicit migration policy.

Proposed initial tuning: retain approximately the existing eight-member event group while relocating its trigger and spawn arrangement. Final crowd size and pressure still require playtesting, but **very close spawn placement, immediate northward ingress and instant closed-door breach are requirements, not tuning suggestions**.
## 7. Sporting goods store: independent rescue and tactical reward

### Narrative identity

The optional field-run survivor is a different person from Imani at the clinic and Tomas at the depot. Use wording such as **“Another survivor is trapped in the sporting goods store.”** Do not label this survivor as the selected caller.

Update the building sign, map legend, objective text, instructions, interaction/completion messages, practice labels, assisted route and report-facing text. Do not globally replace “clinic” in the narrative or its route identifier.

### Access and guard group

Place a deliberately authored large group around the store's front doorway. The group must exist in every threat setting: Guided, Standard, Tense, Dangerous and Nightmare. Do not rely on random ambient placement or allow a low active-enemy budget to remove the encounter.

Keep the front door locked. **Exactly two windows are player entry routes.** Intact windows block entry; either window can be broken using the established shooting or Search / Use methods, and its broken opening must actually become traversable.

Breaking a store window sends the storefront group toward that window's position. An active car alarm has higher priority, allowing the player to break a window while the horde remains diverted. The alarm does not unlock the building: even the alarm strategy still requires using a window.

Do not let a generic door-open or zombie-door-break routine create an unintended third entrance. Do not make a supposedly decorative counter, rack or broken window into an invisible blockage.

Proposed initial tuning: at least eight authored storefront guards, independent of the ambient placement seed, with actual crowd size/pressure verified in the lowest and highest threat settings. This count is a tuning proposal; the requested invariant is a large group in every mode.

### Shotgun reward

Place a collectible shotgun inside the store, with **eight shells total** on collection. Award it once. Entering the store and collecting the weapon is the reward condition; do not silently make rescue completion an extra prerequisite. Rescue remains optional and retains its existing benefits.

The requested spread is **30 degrees total**, centered on the facing/aim direction: **−15° to +15°**. Each trigger pull spends one shell, not one shell per pellet. Keep separate ammunition for the existing gun and the shotgun.

Use finite-range projectiles/pellets with proper wall, car and window obstruction. It must be possible to hit multiple enemies in the spread, but the weapon must not damage every enemy in an unlimited sector or penetrate intact walls. Tune pellet count, range, damage and cadence explicitly before implementation; these values were not supplied by the user.

The existing ammo pouch, general ammo pickups, threat pickup bonuses and Heavy Carbine damage must not silently inflate the eight-shell shotgun reward or its damage. Preserve the purchased primary gun when switching.

Because Nightfall now has two usable weapons, a weapon-switch control is mandatory.

Add a clearly labeled **weapon switch / change weapon** touch button and a documented, non-conflicting keyboard binding. The control must be available in live play and DEV/practice on supported devices. Show selected weapon and its ammunition. Preserve weapon ownership, selected weapon, shell count and pickup state across pause/recovery. No free refills on switching or reconnect.

### Assisted and developer modes

Developer practice uses the same encounter and reward rules. In assisted mode, present explicit guarded-door, diversion and window-entry choices rather than requiring action controls or granting the rescue/shotgun immediately. The exact choice costs/timing are a proposed design detail to resolve without changing academic evidence or forcing a new action run.

For Nightfall, include the highest threat setting, the storefront group, garage ambush, an active alarm and shotgun effects together. Optimize rendering/audio work rather than silently deleting required groups, shortening effects or removing mechanics on phones.

**Live-run timing distinction:** backgrounding freezes unsafe local simulation/input, not the entire team's deadline. ## 17. Resource-market redesign — remove the dead-end credit economy

The current equipment economy creates a false strategic choice.

Current source exposes:

- different item prices;
- a **Save the credits / Save all credits** choice;
- ending text reporting unused credits;
- Nightfall's supply-contract credits.

The design problem is that unused credits have no meaningful later purpose. A cautious student can reasonably infer that saving currency matters, only to discover that the session ends without another use for it.

### v0.9.2 design decision

**Remove the student-facing dead-end credit economy.**

For both current cartridges:

- remove the option to save credits for a later purchase that never arrives;
- remove leftover-credit reporting from the ending;
- remove arbitrary price differences as the main basis of the equipment decision;
- replace "purchasing" with direct equipment unlocks tied to completed mathematics.

If legacy numeric currency fields must temporarily remain internally for migration compatibility, they must not be exposed as a meaningful student strategy and must not drive the new unlock model.

### New preparation loop

The intended loop becomes:

**complete required math → choose one resource → optionally complete more math → unlock another resource → optionally complete more math → unlock the third resource → play the action game**

For cartridges with three current equipment options:

1. Required progression through the preparation stage grants **one equipment slot**.
2. A completed optional extension block grants **a second distinct equipment slot**.
3. A second completed optional extension block grants **a third distinct equipment slot**.
4. The same item cannot occupy more than one slot.
5. The team may proceed after one or two resources if it chooses not to extend further.
6. All earned/selected team resources must propagate to every eligible student's individual run.

Current Nightfall team resources:

- Heavy Carbine
- Ammo Pouch
- Protective Vest

Current Vault 7 resource concepts:

- Code Scanner
- Silent Toolkit
- Cloak

The cartridge-specific effects remain local; this section changes the **acquisition model**, not their mechanics.

### Academic evidence separation

Extension questions remain normal mathematics. Their answers and first-attempt evidence continue to be recorded normally.

The equipment unlock is a gameplay reward for completing the extension block. It must not overwrite or inflate academic mastery.

## 20. Nightfall — current cartridge design direction

Nightfall is becoming the stronger gameplay draw and should receive the larger share of polish in v0.9.2.

Its desired structure is:

**required mathematics → consequential rescue-route choice → more mathematics → equipment preparation → optional extra mathematics for additional equipment → difficult individual field run → final crew decision → narrative payoff**

The clinic-versus-depot choice already affects the narrative and should remain.

The action game should remain **meaningfully difficult**. Universal completion is not the target.

A desirable classroom result may be that only one or a few students successfully clear a hard initial run, provided:

- failure is understandable rather than arbitrary;
- tactics matter;
- equipment meaningfully improves odds;
- a student can identify something to do differently next time;
- arcade failure does not damage academic evidence.

Do **not** automatically nerf Nightfall simply because many students fail their first run.

Desired student reaction is closer to:

**"I want another chance; I know what I could do differently."**

Useful tactical lessons should include things such as:

- earn another equipment slot;
- conserve ammunition;
- use car alarms intelligently;
- use windows/alternate entrances;
- avoid unnecessary fights;
- exploit the sporting-goods-store risk/reward route when worthwhile.

### Classroom duration

MathQuest's lesson length can be controlled primarily through the number of required questions, gates and optional extensions.

Do not artificially lengthen every action game merely to occupy a full class period.

## 21. Cross-cartridge design rules learned from Vault 7 and Nightfall

Carry the following principles into future cartridge specifications.

### 21.1 Gate choices should visibly matter

A gate decision need not create a completely separate game, but a student should be able to tell what changed.

Useful consequences include:

- different ending/narrative state;
- altered route or layout;
- different tactical risk;
- different objective;
- different ally/resource;
- changed enemy/environment state.

Avoid dramatic-looking decisions that have almost no later consequence.

### 21.2 Preparation should matter

Resources/power-ups should create noticeable tactical advantages.

A cartridge may remain technically completable with minimal equipment, but optional preparation should not feel cosmetic.

### 21.3 Math success and arcade success remain separate

A student may complete the mathematics successfully and still fail the action game.

That is acceptable and is part of the intended MathQuest structure.

### 21.4 Hard but fair is a valid reward-game target

Difficulty can create replay desire and classroom excitement.

The design requirement is fairness and learnability, not universal success.

## 27. Shared mathematics — simplified fractions only where relevant

**Requirement ID:** MATH-FRAC-01  
**Added:** Consolidated Draft 4 · 22 September 2026  
**Source:** Will's instruction: “Also let's make sure only simplified fractions are accepted where relevant across the mathematics modules.”  
**Status:** Required for the next implementation; implementation audit and application acceptance tests not run.

### 27.1 Acceptance rule and scope

**When a relevant final answer is given as a fraction, it must have the correct value and be in simplest form. Mathematical equivalence alone is not enough.**

Apply this consistently across all fraction-capable MathQuest mathematics modules and all supported difficulty levels, not only a module named Fraction Simplification. Audit fraction arithmetic, mixed-number answers, conversion-to-fraction tasks and any other supported task with a fractional numerical answer. This list is an audit scope, not a claim that each named task exists in the current module catalog.

The rule covers both existing and future MathQuest cartridges, ordinary gates, teacher-added extension questions, optional additional-question/reward paths and supported imported/custom questions. New modules must use the same acceptance contract rather than silently accepting unreduced equivalents.

This is a prospective validation requirement. No example of the current application accepting an unreduced fraction has been supplied in this update, and no such defect is claimed to have been reproduced. The earlier 22-iPad export exercised Integer Operations only; it supplies no fraction-validation evidence. Do not relabel that test as verification of this requirement.

### 27.2 Preserve the intended representation and learning task

“Where relevant” must be determined from the item/module response contract, not merely from finding a slash in text.

- For a final fraction answer, require lowest terms. For an allowed mixed-number answer, require a reduced fractional part and a proper fractional part under the existing mixed-number contract.
- Simplest form does **not** by itself mandate mixed numbers instead of improper fractions. Preserve whichever forms the question explicitly permits. For example, `7/3` is reduced; `2 1/3` may also be accepted when the item's format allows it. `14/6` is not reduced, and `2 2/6` has an unreduced fractional part.
- Preserve valid integer entry when the result is a whole number. Do not require students to enter a denominator to submit `2` when the accepted result is the integer 2. A reducible equivalent such as `6/3` must not pass in place of the simplified answer. Whether `2/1` is permitted is governed by the existing answer-format contract; this update does not invent a new denominator-one ban. Similarly, `0` remains a valid zero where allowed; `0/5` is unreduced, while `0/1` is reduced if fraction notation is permitted.
- Decimal, percent, unit and other required answer formats remain as specified by their own questions. A fraction-only prompt must not be bypassed by an equivalent decimal; a decimal-answer prompt must not suddenly demand a fraction. The requirement concerns numerical fraction answers and supported fractional answer components. It does not authorize adding an unrestricted symbolic-algebra simplifier or changing unrelated scoring rules.

### 27.3 Validate the submitted form before reduction

The authoritative grading path must retain the student's original submitted representation. For a numeric fraction `n/d`, parse supported integer components safely, reject a zero denominator, and check `gcd(abs(n), abs(d)) == 1` on the **submitted** numerator and denominator before fraction reduction can erase whether the student simplified it.

A normalized/reduced copy may be used for exact value comparison, but it must not replace the raw representation for this test. Avoid floating-point approximations for exact fraction equality. Preserve the supported sign conventions; normalizing a sign or whitespace is not a reason to bypass the greatest-common-divisor check. Invalid signs or malformed components follow the existing input contract.

For mixed numbers, inspect the submitted fractional component before converting the whole response to an improper fraction. Otherwise an input such as `2 2/4` can be silently normalized to `5/2` and wrongly accepted as simplified. Preserve the established interpretation of negative mixed numbers and test it explicitly.

Use one shared acceptance policy across module-specific validators. Apply it in the authoritative server/answer-checking path as well as any client-side feedback or preview; a browser-only restriction is insufficient. The exact function names and metadata fields must be selected after inspecting the actual repository source, rather than invented from this document.

Audit the full chain:

**item response contract → displayed instructions → raw input / serialization → normalization → authoritative grading → accepted result / feedback → gate and extension progression → recorded attempt and report.**

No keypad, keyboard, import path, extension generator or API submission may turn a valid-but-unreduced fraction into an accepted answer by automatically simplifying it before the relevant check.

### 27.4 Feedback, progression and academic records

Show an instruction beside relevant response fields, such as **“Give your answer in simplest form.”** Preserve clear stacked-fraction rendering and supported whole-number entry on desktop, iPad and iPhone.

Use that message only when equivalence has been established. A different numerical value must not receive misleading “value is correct” feedback. Preserve the student's entry so it can be edited; do not auto-fill the simplified answer.

An unreduced submission is **not an accepted answer**. It must not complete the item, satisfy the student's gate contribution or satisfy an extension-completion reward until the student submits an accepted answer. This changes acceptance only; it does not create a new extra penalty or change the established retry/attempt policy.
